During my exchange at ETH Zurich's Multi-Scale Robotics Lab, I investigated how catheter diameter, support stiffness, and surface condition affect insertion force in a simplified breast-duct model. The project was supervised by **Prof. Bradley Nelson** and received a semester-project grade of **5.5/6.0**.

**My role:** I designed and fabricated 0.5-mm and 0.7-mm nitinol-supported catheter prototypes, built and calibrated a four-load-cell force platform with Arduino and Python data acquisition, fabricated the silicone phantom, and performed the mechanical and imaging experiments.

## Key results

- The **0.5-mm silicone-covered catheter** produced the lowest measured peak insertion-axis force: **268.8 mN**.
- This was more than **90% lower** than the 1.1-mm rigid reference in the simplified silicone setup.
- External illumination enabled interpretable channel imaging without increasing distal catheter diameter and preserved visibility of a porcine-blood region.
