During my March–June 2026 semester project at [ETH Zürich](https://ethz.ch/en.html)'s Multi-Scale Robotics Lab, I investigated how nitinol support diameter and surface condition affect insertion force in a simplified silicone breast-duct model. The project was supervised by **Prof. Bradley Nelson** and received a grade of **5.5/6.0**.

**My role:** I designed and fabricated flexible catheter prototypes with **0.5-mm and 0.7-mm nitinol supports**, built and calibrated a four-load-cell force platform with Arduino and Python data acquisition, fabricated the silicone phantom, and performed mechanical and imaging experiments. These dimensions refer to the nitinol supports, not the complete imaging catheter's outer diameter.

## Experimental findings

- Across the tested support diameters and dry, saline-wetted, and silicone-covered configurations, the **0.5-mm nitinol support with silicone covering** gave the lowest reported insertion-axis peak-force measure, **268.8 mN**, in the simplified silicone setup.
- **20 manual trials were recorded per condition; 10 selected trials per condition were analyzed.** The study also included a 1.1-mm rigid reference.
- External illumination improved visibility of the duct-like channel. Imaging tests included conditions without external illumination, with external illumination, and with external illumination plus porcine blood.

These are benchtop prototype and phantom results, rather than clinical validation.
